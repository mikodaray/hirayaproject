The Mythos of Hiraya

Long before there were people, there was only the sea beneath the sky. This is the story of the two gods who ruled them, the children who rebelled, the seed that grew into the first people, and the ten charms, the Agimats, that still carry the wishes of that first age.

How to read this page. Every part is marked with how settled it is.

Canon decided by the creator Draft proposed, not confirmed Open not decided yet

This entry does not include the main characters. It is about the world they will walk into. Names of the people of the islands are spelled the way the source stories spell them.

The big ideas

  • Two forces run through everything. The hope of Maguayan's seed, called hiraya, and the pride of Licalibutan, who attacked the sky. Every wish echoes one or the other. Draft
  • The gods are the patrons of the Agimats. The two strongest Agimats come from Maguayan and Captan. Their children hold the next strongest, then their grandchildren, then the first people. Canon
  • The gods' other children became the world's spirits and monsters. The mythical creatures and beings of Philippine folklore are descendants of the first gods, and some became demigods with powers of their own. Canon
  • Many names, one family. The peoples of the islands each learned one face of this family and gave it their own name. Bathala, Kabunian, and Magbabaya are all names for the sky god. Draft
  • The words matter. Baybayin, the old script, is the language the spirits listen to. Words read aloud in it with real intent are heard. Canon

The age before Canon

Long ago there was only the sea beneath the sky. Maguayan ruled the sea. Captan ruled the sky. There was nothing between them, only empty air, and the two gods faced each other across it. This is the main creation story of the world, "How the World Was Made."

Draft The two are not enemies. The sea reflects the sky and the sky waters the sea. But the space between them is empty and silent, and that emptiness is the problem the story begins with.

The family

Maguayan and Captan had children, Lidagat (the sea) and Lihangin (the wind), who married and had four children of their own: Licalibutan, Liadlao, Libulan, and Lisuga. Canon

Draft personalities, so each can carry meaning later: Draft

  • Licalibutan was the strongest and the proudest. He wanted a place of his own, not just the space between sea and sky.
  • Liadlao was warm and bright, loyal to his brother, and followed with a warm heart and no plan.
  • Libulan was quiet and watchful. He saw where this was going and went anyway, out of loyalty.
  • Lisuga was the silver one, the youngest. She did not want war and was swept up in it.

The rebellion and the lightning Canon

Licalibutan persuaded his brothers to attack Captan's sky. Draft His reasoning was that something that lives between two powers will always be ruled by them, and he wanted to be a power too. His argument was half true, which is why the others listened.

Captan struck them with lightning. Libulan became the moon. Liadlao became the sun. Lisuga's silver body scattered across the sky as the stars. Licalibutan's rocky body broke apart and became the land. The islands of the Sangkapuluan are his pieces.

Draft Captan did not want to destroy his grandchildren. He struck to stop the fall of his sky, and the lightning could not be taken back. What was left was beautiful and broken at once: light, a moon, a field of stars, and wreckage that became land. His judgment is real, and so is his grief.

The seed and the first people Canon

Maguayan planted a seed on Licalibutan's broken body. From a bamboo tree that grew from it came the first people, Sicalac and Sicabay.

The First Wish. Draft The seed was a wish. Maguayan had just lost his grandchildren, and his answer was a hope, not a revenge: that the broken thing could become a home for someone. That hope is hiraya, and the words "hiraya manawari," the wish that your dreams come true, are the first words of that wish. The bamboo is the place where the wish took a body.

Their descendants include Libo, Saman, Pandaguan, and Arion.

The scattering

Pandaguan mistook a huge shark for a god, and then defied the gods. Captan struck him with lightning. Canon Draft Pandaguan's story repeats Licalibutan's pride on a smaller scale. The world now has two paths: the hope of the seed, and the pride of the rebel. The source also says the lightning changed the color of Pandaguan's line. This wiki does not use that part, since it was an old explanation of skin color and does not fit this story. Instead, Open the lightning could leave his line with another kind of mark, such as a scar, a burn, or a streak.

The gods then scattered the peoples across the earth. Canon Draft This is the end of the first age. The gods step back and leave the world to the people. What remains of them is the sun, the moon, the stars, the land, the sea, the sky, and the hope in the seed.

The Agimats Canon

Agimats exist all around the world. They are protective charms with powers of their own. In the Philippines there are ten, and the ancient Filipinos called the islands the Sangkapuluan.

What an Agimat is Draft

An Agimat is a wish made real. It is hiraya, a dream held so strongly by a person or a people that the spirits gave it a body. The body can be a necklace, a tube, a blade, a cloth, a stone, or anything the maker held when they wished. Each Agimat holds exactly one hope, and its power is the shape of that hope.

Who stands behind each one Canon

The gods of the main story are the patrons of the Agimats. The two strongest Agimats come from Maguayan and Captan. Their children, Lidagat and Lihangin, have the next strongest, and so on down the family. A patron god may watch the Agimat and its bearer. How involved they are is Open.

How an Agimat wakes Draft

An Agimat sleeps until two things meet: a bearer whose heart matches its hope, and a moment when that hope is needed. Waking is marked by warmth, a hum, and a faint gold light called the banaag. A sleeping Agimat can be carried for years without anyone knowing.

Hope and pride Draft

An Agimat reflects the wish it was born from. A wish made from love, protection, or hope makes a helper. A wish made from fear, greed, or the wish to rule the sky makes something dangerous, like Licalibutan's pride, and may draw Captan's lightning. The ten are not good or evil by nature, they are shaped by what was wished. This is why not every Agimat is a friend.

The ten Agimats Draft

The family line in the main story has exactly ten names that fit the rule: the two gods, their two children, their four grandchildren, and the two first people. That is 2 + 2 + 4 + 2, and it matches the ten. Rank 1 is the strongest. The order inside a rank is Open.

Beings and demigods Canon

The children of the first gods became mythical creatures and beings, and some of them became demigods with powers of their own. Canon The folklore names only a few children of the gods. Canon The gods and their children had many more, and those unnamed children became the creatures and spirits of the islands, with a few growing into demigods.

The ladder of power Draft

  1. The gods: Maguayan and Captan, and their Agimats.
  2. Their children: Lidagat and Lihangin, and their Agimats.
  3. The grandchildren: Licalibutan, Liadlao, Libulan, Lisuga, and their Agimats.
  4. The first people: Sicalac and Sicabay, and their Agimat.
  5. The demigods: strong children of the gods and their children, with powers but not one of the ten Agimats.
  6. The creatures and beings: the many other children, spread through the sea, land, sky, and the places between.
  7. People: descendants of the first people, who can bear an Agimat when it chooses them.

Three kinds of children Draft

Folklore describes three kinds of diwata: nature spirits bound to places, humanlike spirits who are not bound (the engkanto), and harmful beings such as the aswang. That fits the two forces of the mythos.

  • The bound ones stayed with their place and became its guardian: a river spirit, a mountain spirit, a tree spirit. They follow hope and care.
  • The unbound ones walked among people and took human shape or voice. They are neither good nor evil and can help or harm.
  • The dark kin followed pride, like Licalibutan, and turned into things that prey on people.

The nine lineages Draft

Every being in Philippine folklore is placed under the god it descends from, by domain first, then by any tie the folklore already states, then by behavior. Select a lineage to see its beings in the glossary. The counts update from the glossary data.

A few beings sit between two lines, such as Apung Malyari (moon and mountain), Aring Sinukuan (sun and mountain), Oryol (river and pride), and Manaul (sky and the bamboo). These cross line beings are the places for conflict or marriage inside the family. Beings from later religions, such as angels and jinn, are not placed in the family. Open

Many names, one family Draft

Across the islands, peoples named the same gods in different ways. In this universe that is literally true. The Tagalog, Ilocano, Bisaya, and other peoples each learned one face of the same family and gave it their own name. The Bisaya story is the one that already names the whole family.

Our godOther names in folklore

Real folklore calls many of these figures supreme gods in their own right. In this universe they are the local faces of the same family, and the gods' children are the demigods such as Makiling, Lumawig, and the Hinilawod heroes. How the people in each age and place see the gods is Open.

The Swallowers and other threats Draft

Several folklore beings hunger for the sun and the moon. The Swallowers are the sea and sky serpents, dragons, and birds that try to eat what the lightning made.

  • Bakunawa, the sea serpent that swallows the moons. In Bicolano and Panay myth it is a spurned sea goddess.
  • Sawa and Olimaw, serpents that try to swallow the moon. Arimaonga, the lion behind lunar eclipses. Samal Naga, a dragon trapped in the Milky Way.
  • Minokawa, the giant bird with a steel beak that devours the sun and the moon.

The moon swallowers are placed in Lidagat's line, because the tides answer to the moon and some of Lidagat's children hunger for it. A possible largest threat is someone gathering Agimats to repeat Licalibutan's attack on the sky.

Rules of the magic

Baybayin and speech Canon

Baybayin is the old Filipino script and the key to the mythos. Draft It is the language the spirits listen to. Words read aloud in it, with real intent, are heard, like a wish said to someone who is actually there. Writing alone does nothing. Reading aloud is the trigger, and the intent of the reader matters. Music can join the reading, because voice and music are both ways of being heard. Lihangin, the wind, is the child of the gods who could carry spoken words.

The cost Open

Magic with no cost makes a weak story. Options still on the table: the more the bearer uses an Agimat, the more of the hope's price they pay (memory, warmth, time); an Agimat only works when the bearer acts for someone else; or using one too long pulls the bearer toward the beginning of the world.

Respect Draft

In folklore people said "tabi po" to ask a spirit's permission when passing its place. A possible rule: Agimats are asked, not taken.

Home and return Open

A natural answer inside this mythos is that the way back opens only when the hope an Agimat was born from is answered. That would give a long story a clear goal.

Other lands Canon

Agimats exist all around the world. Every land can have its own first story and its own first wish, so other countries can have their own creation stories and Agimats later without breaking this one.
