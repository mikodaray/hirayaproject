# Core truths of the mythos

## A. Locked facts (DECIDED by the creator)
1. Agimats exist all around the world. They are protective charms with powers of their own.
2. There are 10 Agimats in the Philippines. Ancient Filipinos called the islands the Sangkapuluan.
3. Jeff's necklace is one of the ten. His father gave it to him when he turned 18.
4. Baybayin, the old Filipino script, is the portal key. Reading the inscription on the bamboo tube aloud sends the four to Butuan.
5. The words read at the end of the chapter 1 reading are "Hiraya manawari", the wish that your dreams come true.
6. The four arrive in Butuan in the year 1003 (arc 1 covers 1001 to 1009). History is used as flavor, the story comes first.
7. The main creation story of this universe is "How the World Was Made", with Maguayan (sea) and Captan (sky) as the main gods (decided 2026-11-10). See 06-the-gods-and-creation.md.
8. The gods of the main story are the patrons of the Agimats. The two strongest Agimats come from Maguayan and Captan, their children have the next strongest, and so on (decided 2026-11-10). See 02-the-ten-agimats.md.
9. The children of the first gods became mythical creatures and beings, and some became demigods with powers (decided 2026-11-10). See 07-beings-and-demigods.md.
10. The people of Butuan speak an older Bisaya. The four speak Bisaya at home. All dialogue on the page is English.

## C. The proposed mythos (all DRAFT, built on the main creation story in 06-the-gods-and-creation.md)

### C1. What an Agimat is
An Agimat is a wish made real. It is hiraya, an imagination or dream held so strongly by a person or a people that the spirits gave it a body. The body can be a necklace, a tube, a blade, a cloth, a stone, or anything the maker held when they wished. Each Agimat holds one hope, and its power is the shape of that hope.

Why this fits: the series title is Hiraya, and the chapter title is Banaag, a first glimmer of light. An Agimat is the first glimmer of a dream becoming real.

### C2. Where the ten come from (DECIDED in principle, mapping is DRAFT)
The ten Agimats are patroned by the gods and their family from the main creation story. The two strongest come from Maguayan and Captan, the next strongest from their children, then the grandchildren, then the first people. See 02-the-ten-agimats.md for the ten, by patron and rank.

The core idea: an Agimat holds a wish, and a wish echoes either Maguayan's seed (hope) or Licalibutan's attack on the sky (pride).

### C3. How an Agimat wakes
An Agimat sleeps until two things meet: a bearer whose heart matches its hope, and a moment when that hope is needed. Waking is marked by warmth, a hum, and a faint gold light (the banaag). A sleeping Agimat can be carried for years without anyone knowing, which is why Jeff has worn his without feeling anything until chapter 1.

### C4. Baybayin and speech
Baybayin is the language the spirits listen to. Words read aloud in it, with real intent, are heard. Writing alone does nothing. Reading aloud is the trigger, and the intent of the reader matters.
- Music joins the reading because voice and music are both ways of being heard.
- Lihangin, the wind, is the child of the gods who could carry spoken words (DRAFT idea from the main story).

### C5. The tube
In the main creation story the first people came out of a bamboo tree that grew from Maguayan's seed. So the bamboo tube is a piece of that bamboo, the cradle of the first people, and it carries the first words in Baybayin. Whether the tube is itself one of the ten is OPEN (see 02-the-ten-agimats.md for the options).

### C6. The necklace and Butuan
The necklace was woken in Butuan long ago. Someone carried it out of that time and it passed down the years to Jeff's father. The four were pulled back to its birthplace because its hope is needed there again in 1003. Jeff follows a thread of light to the cabinet and the people he needs. This also explains why the court recognizes it.

### C7. Not all Agimats are friendly
An Agimat reflects the wish it was born from. A wish made from love, protection, or hope makes a helper. A wish made from fear, greed, or the wish to rule the sky makes something dangerous, like Licalibutan's pride, and may draw Captan's lightning. The ten are not good or evil by nature, they are shaped by what was wished. This sets up conflict without making anyone a flat villain, and it fits the elder's warning. A possible final antagonist: someone gathering Agimats to repeat Licalibutan's attack on the sky.

### C8. Limits and cost (OPEN, important)
Magic with no cost makes a weak story. Options:
- The more the bearer uses it, the more of the hope's price they pay (memory, warmth, time).
- An Agimat only works when the bearer is acting for someone else.
- Using it too long pulls the bearer toward the beginning, which could be how the four stay stuck in the past.
Pick one or write your own.

### C9. Home and return (OPEN)
How do the four go home? A natural answer within this mythos is that the hope of the necklace must be fulfilled, so the way home opens only when the wish it was born from is answered. This gives the whole arc a goal.

### C10. Other lands
Agimats exist all around the world (DECIDED). Every land can have its own first story and its own first wish, so other countries can have their own creation stories and their own Agimats later, without breaking this one.

## D. Spirits and beings (OPEN)
The mythos will probably include the diwata and anito (see 03-folklore-sources.md). Decide how visible they are: hidden and felt only (spooky, subtle), or seen and spoken to (fantasy, adventurous).
