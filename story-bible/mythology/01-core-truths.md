# Core truths of the mythos

## A. Locked facts (DECIDED by the creator)
1. Agimats exist all around the world. They are protective charms with powers of their own.
2. There are 10 Agimats in the Philippines. Ancient Filipinos called the islands the Sangkapuluan.
3. Jeff's necklace is one of the ten. His father gave it to him when he turned 18.
4. Baybayin, the old Filipino script, is the portal key. Reading the inscription on the bamboo tube aloud sends the four to Butuan.
5. The words read at the end of the chapter 1 reading are "Hiraya manawari", the wish that your dreams come true.
6. The four arrive in Butuan in the year 1003 (arc 1 covers 1001 to 1009). History is used as flavor, the story comes first.
7. The main creation story of this universe is "How the World Was Made", with Maguayan (sea) and Captan (sky) as the main gods (decided 2026-11-10). See 06-the-gods-and-creation.md.
8. The people of Butuan speak an older Bisaya. The four speak Bisaya at home. All dialogue on the page is English.

## B. Facts already shown in the chapter 1 draft (DRAFT, need confirmation)
- The necklace warms, glows, and hums when it is near the tube.
- The tube has Baybayin carvings with faint gold light inside them, and it hums the same way the necklace does.
- The tube disappears when the four land in Butuan, so the way home is unknown.
- The elder in Butuan knows the necklace is "one of the ten" and says "Then it woke in my land for a reason."
- The elder warns that not all ten Agimats sleep, and not all are friends.
- Jeff can read some Baybayin because he studies it for fun.
- Music (Mel's violin) takes part in the reading and calms people in Butuan.

## C. The proposed mythos (all DRAFT, built on the creation stories in 05-creation-stories.md)

### C1. What an Agimat is
An Agimat is a wish made real. It is hiraya, an imagination or dream held so strongly by a person or a people that the spirits gave it a body. The body can be a necklace, a tube, a blade, a cloth, a stone, or anything the maker held when they wished. Each Agimat holds one hope, and its power is the shape of that hope.

Why this fits: the series title is Hiraya, and the chapter title is Banaag, a first glimmer of light. An Agimat is the first glimmer of a dream becoming real.

### C2. The First Wish (the origin of the ten, DRAFT, creator to confirm)
The base is the main creation story, "How the World Was Made" (DECIDED, see 06-the-gods-and-creation.md). Beats marked with a story number come from the stories in 05-creation-stories.md. Everything else is our own addition.
1. In the beginning there was only sea and sky. Maguayan is the main god of the sea and Captan is the main god of the sky. (story 1, DECIDED)
2. Their grandchildren rebelled. Proud Licalibutan led the others against the sky, and Captan answered with lightning. The sun, the moon, the stars, and the land came from the wreckage. The land is the broken body of Licalibutan, and the islands are his pieces, the Sangkapuluan. (story 1)
3. Maguayan planted a seed in the broken land, and from a bamboo tree came the first people, Sicalac and Sicabay. (story 1) Our addition: the seed was a wish, the First Wish, that the broken world would live again. This first hope is hiraya, and "hiraya manawari" is its first words.
4. The first people lived together at a first home, like Cibolan on Mount Apo. When hard times came (a drought in story 4), the children had to scatter, and each pair carried one thing from the first home. (story 4) Our addition: each of those things held a piece of the First Wish. Ten of them became the ten Agimats of the Sangkapuluan, and each shaped the people who carried it.
5. Our addition: the Agimats went to sleep, one by one, as their people settled and forgot.

Other options for the ten, if you do not like step 4 (OPEN):
- Option B, the ten pieces of Licalibutan. When he broke apart, ten pieces kept a spark of him, which means the ten Agimats are tied to pride and to the land itself.
- Option C, ten gifts from Maguayan and Captan after the lightning, as an apology or a promise.
All three options keep Maguayan and Captan as the main gods.

### C3. How an Agimat wakes
An Agimat sleeps until two things meet: a bearer whose heart matches its hope, and a moment when that hope is needed. Waking is marked by warmth, a hum, and a faint gold light (the banaag). A sleeping Agimat can be carried for years without anyone knowing, which is why Jeff has worn his without feeling anything until chapter 1.

### C4. Baybayin and speech
Baybayin is the language the spirits listen to. Words read aloud in it, with real intent, are heard. Writing alone does nothing. Reading aloud is the trigger, and the intent of the reader matters.
- In story 3 the sky was once close to the ground. In our version, reading the right words aloud makes the sky close again for a moment, and the world answers. That is the portal.
- In story 7 a speaking bird is the first voice. A speaking bird or a bird spirit could be a messenger in later chapters.
- Music joins the reading because voice and music are both ways of being heard.

### C5. The tube
First people came out of bamboo in the stories (1 and 8), and out of reeds in story 2. So the bamboo tube is a piece of the first bamboo, the cradle of the first people, and it carries the first words in Baybayin. It is the door to the first age, and it opens only for a reader whose Agimat is awake. Whether the tube is itself one of the ten is OPEN. A neat answer is that it is not an Agimat but the thing the First Wish grew from, so it can send people back toward the beginning.

### C6. The necklace and Butuan
The necklace was woken in Butuan long ago. Someone carried it out of that time and it passed down the years to Jeff's father. The four were pulled back to its birthplace because its hope is needed there again in 1003. Like the man who follows a floating hair upstream in story 7, Jeff follows a thread of light to the cabinet and the people he needs. This also explains why the court recognizes it.

### C7. Not all Agimats are friendly
An Agimat reflects the wish it was born from. A wish made from love, protection, or hope makes a helper. A wish made from fear, greed, or the wish to rule the sky makes something dangerous, like Licalibutan's pride. The ten are not good or evil by nature, they are shaped by what was wished. This sets up conflict without making anyone a flat villain, and it fits the elder's warning. A possible final antagonist: someone gathering Agimats to repeat Licalibutan's attack on the sky.

### C8. Limits and cost (OPEN, important)
Magic with no cost makes a weak story. Options:
- The more the bearer uses it, the more of the hope's price they pay (memory, warmth, time).
- An Agimat only works when the bearer is acting for someone else.
- Using it too long pulls the bearer toward the First Wish, the beginning, which could be how the four stay stuck in the past.
Pick one or write your own.

### C9. Home and return (OPEN)
How do the four go home? A natural answer within this mythos is that the hope of the necklace must be fulfilled, so the way home opens only when the wish it was born from is answered. This gives the whole arc a goal.

### C10. Other lands
Agimats exist all around the world (DECIDED). Every land has its own first story and its own first wish, so other countries can have their own creation stories and their own Agimats later, without breaking this one.
