# Core truths of the mythos

## A. Locked facts (DECIDED by the creator)
1. Agimats exist all around the world. They are protective charms with powers of their own.
2. There are 10 Agimats in the Philippines. Ancient Filipinos called the islands the Sangkapuluan.
3. Jeff's necklace is one of the ten. His father gave it to him when he turned 18.
4. Baybayin, the old Filipino script, is the portal key. Reading the inscription on the bamboo tube aloud sends the four to Butuan.
5. The words read at the end of the chapter 1 reading are "Hiraya manawari", the wish that your dreams come true.
6. The four arrive in Butuan in the year 1003 (arc 1 covers 1001 to 1009). History is used as flavor, the story comes first.
7. The people of Butuan speak an older Bisaya. The four speak Bisaya at home. All dialogue on the page is English.

## B. Facts already shown in the chapter 1 draft (DRAFT, need confirmation)
- The necklace warms, glows, and hums when it is near the tube.
- The tube has Baybayin carvings with faint gold light inside them, and it hums the same way the necklace does.
- The tube disappears when the four land in Butuan, so the way home is unknown.
- The elder in Butuan knows the necklace is "one of the ten" and says "Then it woke in my land for a reason."
- The elder warns that not all ten Agimats sleep, and not all are friends.
- Jeff can read some Baybayin because he studies it for fun.
- Music (Mel's violin) takes part in the reading and calms people in Butuan.

## C. The proposed mythos (all DRAFT)

### C1. What an Agimat is
An Agimat is a wish made real. It is hiraya, an imagination or dream held so strongly by a person or a people that the spirits gave it a body. The body can be a necklace, a tube, a blade, a cloth, a stone, or anything the maker held when they wished. Each Agimat holds exactly one hope, and its power is the shape of that hope.

Why this fits: the series title is Hiraya, and the chapter title is Banaag, a first glimmer of light. An Agimat is the first glimmer of a dream becoming real.

### C2. Where the ten come from (OPEN, pick one)
- Option 1, the wishes of the first people (my recommendation). When the islands were first settled, ten people or ten communities made a wish at a turning point (a flood, a famine, a war). The diwata answered each wish with an Agimat. This ties the ten to Butuan era Filipinos and makes the elder's line "it woke in my land" natural.
- Option 2, a gift from the sky or sea. A great being gave the islands ten gifts to guard them. This is more mythic and gives the story a "god" level above the people.
- Option 3, the islands themselves. The Sangkapuluan (islands) are alive in the old belief, and each Agimat is the islands' own heart beating in one place.

### C3. How an Agimat wakes
An Agimat sleeps until two things meet: a bearer whose heart matches its hope, and a moment when that hope is needed. Waking is marked by warmth, a hum, and a faint gold light (the banaag). A sleeping Agimat can be carried for years without anyone knowing, which is why Jeff has worn his without feeling anything until chapter 1.

### C4. Baybayin and speech
Baybayin is the language the spirits listen to. Words read aloud in it, with real intent, are heard, like a wish said to someone who is actually there. Writing alone does nothing. Reading aloud is the trigger, and the intent of the reader matters. This is why the tube worked when Jeff read with the others standing by, and why music can join in.

### C5. The tube
The tube is a "caller": an object that carries a message in Baybayin so that the right reader can speak it at the right time. Whether the tube is itself one of the ten is OPEN. A neat answer is that the tube is not an Agimat but was made by one (the necklace), because the necklace called the four to the cabinet.

### C6. The necklace and Butuan
The necklace was woken in Butuan long ago. Someone carried it out of that time and it passed down the years to Jeff's father. The four were pulled back to its birthplace because its hope is needed there again in 1003. This explains why the court recognizes it.

### C7. Not all Agimats are friendly
An Agimat reflects the wish it was born from. A wish made from love, protection, or hope makes a helper. A wish made from fear, greed, or revenge makes something dangerous. The ten are not good or evil by nature, they are shaped by what was wished. This sets up conflict without making anyone a flat villain, and it fits the elder's warning.

### C8. Limits and cost (OPEN, important)
Magic with no cost makes a weak story. Options:
- The more the bearer uses it, the more of the hope's price they pay (memory, warmth, time).
- An Agimat only works when the bearer is acting for someone else.
- Using it too long pulls the bearer toward the Agimat's origin, which could be how the four stay stuck in the past.
Pick one or write your own.

### C9. Home and return (OPEN)
How do the four go home? A natural answer within this mythos is that the hope of the necklace must be fulfilled, so the way home opens only when the wish it was born from is answered. This gives the whole arc a goal.
