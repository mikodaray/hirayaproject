# The gods and the main creation story

## DECIDED by the creator (2026-11-10)
The story "How the World Was Made" is the main creation story of this universe. Maguayan and Captan are the main gods. Source for the story: https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/creation-phil.html (names are spelled the way the page spells them).

Our one change, also decided: we do not use the ending of the source story that explains the skin colors of peoples. It does not fit this story.

## The main creation story (from the source, shortened)
1. Long ago there was only the sea beneath the sky. Maguayan ruled the sea. Captan ruled the sky.
2. Their children, Lidagat (the sea) and Lihangin (the wind), married and had four children: Licalibutan, Liadlao, Libulan, and Lisuga.
3. Licalibutan was the strongest and the proudest. He persuaded his brothers to attack Captan's sky.
4. Captan struck them with lightning. Libulan became the moon. Liadlao became the sun. Licalibutan's rocky body broke apart and became the land. Lisuga's silver body scattered across the sky as the stars.
5. Maguayan planted a seed on Licalibutan's broken body. From a bamboo tree came the first people, Sicalac and Sicabay.
6. Their descendants include Libo, Saman, Pandaguan, and Arion. Pandaguan mistook a huge shark for a god and then defied the gods, and Captan struck him down with lightning. The gods then scattered the peoples across the earth.

## The expanded telling (DRAFT, built only from the main story)
Lines marked (source) come from the story page. Everything else is a new detail for the creator to confirm, change, or cut. Nothing here uses the other creation stories.

### Part 1, the age before
There was only the sea beneath the sky, and nothing between them. Maguayan lay below and Captan rose above, and the two faced each other across empty air. (source: sea and sky only, Maguayan rules the sea, Captan rules the sky)
DRAFT: they are not enemies. The sea reflects the sky and the sky waters the sea. The space between them is empty and silent, and that emptiness is the problem the story begins with.

### Part 2, the children and the four
Maguayan and Captan's children, Lidagat (the sea) and Lihangin (the wind), married and had four children: Licalibutan, Liadlao, Libulan, and Lisuga. (source)
DRAFT personalities, so each one can carry meaning later:
- Licalibutan: the strongest and proudest. (source) He is the one who wants a place of his own, not just the space between sea and sky.
- Liadlao: warm and bright, loyal to his brother, who follows the others with a warm heart and no plan.
- Libulan: quiet and watchful, who sees where this is going and goes anyway out of loyalty.
- Lisuga: the silver one, the youngest, who does not want war and is swept up in it.

### Part 3, the rebellion
Licalibutan persuaded his brothers to attack Captan's sky. (source)
DRAFT motive: Licalibutan believes that something that lives between two powers will always be ruled by them. He wants to be a power too. His argument is half true, which is why the others listen.

### Part 4, the lightning
Captan struck the brothers with lightning. (source) Libulan became the moon, Liadlao became the sun, Licalibutan's rocky body broke apart to form land, and Lisuga's silver body scattered as the stars. (source)
DRAFT: Captan did not want to destroy his grandchildren. He struck to stop the fall of his sky, and the lightning could not be taken back. What was left was beautiful and broken at once: light, a moon, a field of stars, and wreckage that became land. Captan's judgment is real, and so is his grief.

### Part 5, the seed
Maguayan planted a seed on Licalibutan's broken body. From a bamboo tree came the first people, Sicalac and Sicabay. (source)
DRAFT: the seed is the First Wish. Maguayan has just lost his grandchildren, and his answer is a hope, not a revenge: that the broken thing could become home to someone. This hope is hiraya, and the words "hiraya manawari" are the first words of that wish. The bamboo is the place where the wish took a body.

### Part 6, the first people and the line of Pandaguan
Their descendants include Libo, Saman, Pandaguan, and Arion. (source) Pandaguan mistook a huge shark for a god, then defied the gods, and Captan struck him with lightning. (source, shortened)
DRAFT: Pandaguan's story repeats Licalibutan's pride on a smaller scale. The world has two paths: the hope of the seed, and the pride of the rebel. Skip the source's explanation that the lightning blackened his line, which was a skin color explanation. Instead, the lightning leaves Pandaguan's line with a mark of another kind (a scar, a burn, a streak), see the ideas section below.

### Part 7, the scattering
The gods scattered the peoples across the earth. (source)
DRAFT: the end of the first age. The gods step back from the world and leave it to the people. What remains of them are the sun, the moon, the stars, the land, the sea, the sky, and the hope in the seed.

## What this means for the mythos (DRAFT)
- Two forces run through every story: the hope of Maguayan's seed (hiraya) and the pride of Licalibutan (the attack on the sky). Every Agimat is born from a wish, and a wish echoes one or the other. A wish of hope makes a helper. A wish of pride makes something dangerous. This is why the elder warns that not all the ten are friends.
- Captan's lightning is judgment on pride. In the story, storms and lightning can show that the sky is watching, and a bearer whose wish comes from pride might draw it.
- The sun, moon, and stars were the siblings, so dawn, night, and starlight can carry meaning. The dawn at Butuan (page 31) can be Liadlao, the quiet night in Butuan (pages 43 to 45) can be Libulan, and the necklace's thread of light can echo Lisuga's silver.
- The Sangkapuluan, the islands, are pieces of Licalibutan, so the land the story walks on remembers pride and the lightning.
- The bamboo tube is a piece of the bamboo that gave the first people, a possible link to the First Wish.

## Where could the ten Agimats come from? (OPEN, choose one, all built from the main story)
- Option A, ten joints of the first bamboo. The bamboo that carried the First Wish had ten joints, and each became an Agimat. They are pieces of hope, and the bamboo tube is a joint or a sibling to them.
- Option B, ten sparks of Licalibutan. When he broke into land, ten sparks of him stayed alive inside ten islands. The Agimats are tied to pride and to place, and each one must decide what to become.
- Option C, ten stars of Lisuga. Ten of the silver stars fell to earth and each one became an Agimat. This ties them to Lisuga and to light, and explains the gold glow (banaag).
- Option D, a mix: ten Agimats that are a mix of hope and pride, built from the joints of the bamboo and the sparks of the broken one together.

## The main gods (DRAFT profiles, only the roles are from the source)

### Maguayan, god of the sea
- From the source: rules the sea, planted the seed that grew the first people.
- DRAFT: the patient one. He answers a broken world with hope, not revenge. His gift to people is the First Wish. He is the source of hiraya, the Agimats, and the idea that dreams can come true.
- OPEN: his look, his voice, how he shows himself, whether he is still awake.

### Captan, god of the sky
- From the source: rules the sky, struck the rebels with lightning, struck Pandaguan, scattered the peoples.
- DRAFT: the guardian and the judge. He is not cruel, but he protects the order of the sky at any cost. He is the reason pride is dangerous in this world and why some Agimats born from pride are feared.
- OPEN: his look, his voice, whether he sees the Agimats as a threat or a promise.

## How the gods fit with the rest of the mythos
- Agimats come from wishes that echo Maguayan's seed (hope) or Licalibutan's attack (pride). Captan's lightning is judgment on pride.
- The diwata and anito from folklore (see 03-folklore-sources.md) can be the spirits that carry out the gods' will and answer wishes. How they relate to the main gods is OPEN.
- Butuan, the Rajah, and the elder (1003) may or may not know these gods by these names. In real history, Butuan blended Hindu, Buddhist, and spirit beliefs (see 03-world-and-history.md). OPEN: whether the people of Butuan call the sky and sea gods Captan and Maguayan, or other names.
