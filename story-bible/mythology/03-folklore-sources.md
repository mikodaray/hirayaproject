## Verified from the Wikipedia article on Anito (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anito)
- VERIFIED: anito means ancestor spirits, nature spirits, and deities in Indigenous Philippine folk religions. The meaning varies by ethnic group.
- VERIFIED: anito is mainly a Luzon term. Visayan groups often say diwata instead.
- VERIFIED: the belief system is animistic. Rocks, trees, animals, people, and natural events all have spirits. Scholars call it Anitism.
- VERIFIED: two main kinds, ancestor spirits (ninuno) and deities and nature spirits (diwata).
- VERIFIED: diwata are non-human spirits tied to places, objects, plants, animals, or natural phenomena. Most are kind or neutral, but they can cause harm if angered.
- VERIFIED: people said "tabi po" to ask permission when passing a diwata's place.
- VERIFIED: Visayans sometimes call diwata "dili ingon nato" (those unlike us).
- VERIFIED: shamans are babaylan (Visayan) or katalonan (Tagalog). They were mostly women, and a diwata was said to choose the shaman.
- VERIFIED: carved figures (taotao, larawan, ladaw) became sacred only during a ritual (pag-anito).
- The article does not mention Agusan, Butuan, or agimat.
