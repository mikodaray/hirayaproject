# Folklore gods and heroes by lineage

The gods, goddesses, and divine heroes of Philippine folklore, sorted under the family of Maguayan and Captan. The names and descriptions follow the Wikipedia list of Philippine mythological figures, read through a mirror of the page (https://ipfs.ssi.eecc.de/ipns/en.wikipedia-on-ipfs.org/wiki/List_of_Philippine_mythological_figures). Status: DRAFT. The sorting is my proposal.

Coverage: the page has about 600 entries. I sorted the gods and demigods from the groups I could read (Ivatan through Teduray, Maguindanao, Maranao, and Sama-Bajau). I did not list every minor spirit, mortal, or ancestor. Groups missing from my reading, such as Tausug, are not covered yet (see the end).

## The key idea: many names, one family
Across the islands, peoples named the same gods in different ways. In this universe that is true literally. The Tagalog, Ilocano, Bisaya, and other peoples each learned one face of the same family, and gave it their own name. So there are two kinds of entries:
- Another name for a god of the family (same being, a local name).
- A child or descendant of the family, one of the demigods or divine children (a different being).
The Bisaya story we use is the one that already names the family: Kaptan, Maguayan, Lidagat, Lihangin, Licalibutan, Liadlao, Libulan, and Lisuga. (The Bisaya group on the page also lists Manaul, Maguyaen goddess of sea winds, Adlaw the sun, Bulan the moon, Bakunawa, Sidapa, Magyan, Sumpoy, Sappia, and Lalahon.)

## Cross line demigods (a being that sits in two lines)
These are the ones who make conflict or romance inside the family.
- Apung Malyari: Libulan (moon) and Licalibutan (mountain).
- Aring Sinukuan: Liadlao (sun, war) and Licalibutan (mountain Arayat).
- Dapu and Lakandanup: Lidagat (river and sea) and Libulan (the eclipse).
- Oryol and her father Asuang: Lidagat (river) and Licalibutan (pride).
- Manaul: Captan (sky) and Maguayan (the bamboo).
