# Mythical beings and demigods

## DECIDED by the creator (2026-11-10)
The children of the first gods become mythical creatures and beings. Some of them have become demigods and have powers.

## How I read that (DRAFT, creator to confirm)
The source names only two children of Maguayan and Captan (Lidagat and Lihangin), and four grandchildren (Licalibutan, Liadlao, Libulan, Lisuga). My reading is:
- The named children carry the ten Agimats (see 02-the-ten-agimats.md), along with the gods and the first people.
- The gods and the named children also had many other children, whom the source does not name. These are the ones who became mythical creatures and beings.
- A few of those children grew so strong that they became demigods with powers of their own.
If you meant something different, such as only the named children becoming creatures, tell me and I will change it.

7. People: descendants of the first people, who can bear an Agimat when it chooses them.

## Three kinds of children (DRAFT, built from the folklore categories)
The Wikipedia article on Anito describes three kinds of diwata: nature spirits bound to places, humanlike spirits who are not bound (engkanto), and malevolent beings such as aswang. (VERIFIED, see 03-folklore-sources.md.) That fits the main story's two forces, hope and pride:
- The bound ones: children who stayed with their place and became its guardian, such as a river spirit, a mountain spirit, or a tree spirit. These are the diwata. They follow the pattern of hope and care.
- The unbound ones: children who walked among people and took human shape or a human voice, such as the engkanto. They are neither good nor evil and can help or harm.
- The dark ones: children who followed pride, like Licalibutan, and turned into things that prey on people, such as the aswang. They are tied to the lightning and to the pride that Captan judged.

## Demigods (OPEN, candidates)
The ones who grew powerful. Each could have their own domain, powers, and agenda.
- The Bakunawa, a moon swallowing serpent, as the demigod of eclipse and the swallowing dark. Possible link to Libulan.
- A storm bird demigod of Captan's line, such as a Minokawa or Sarimanok style being.
- A sea queen or sea lord of Maguayan and Lidagat's line.
- A giant of Licalibutan's line, made of land and stone.
- A guardian of a river, such as the Agusan River, that the Rajah of Butuan relies on.
- An elder spirit who watches over the Sangkapuluan, possibly linked to Tandang Procopio's lightning scar idea in 06-the-gods-and-creation.md.
